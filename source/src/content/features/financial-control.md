## A simple income and expense workflow

Recording a rental payment is a small, repeatable workflow. When the tenant's transfer lands, open the property, add an income transaction with the amount and the date, and choose the rent category. When you pay a plumber, add an expense transaction the same way and choose the maintenance category. Every transaction belongs to one property, so each property's monthly totals follow automatically from what you record.

Here is a concrete example. You own one apartment and receive $1,200 in rent on the first of the month. You add that as income in the rent category. On the tenth, a water heater repair costs $240 and you add it as an expense in the maintenance category. When you open that property's monthly summary, the app shows $1,200 income, $240 expenses, and $960 net income for the month, calculated from the transactions you entered.

## Categories you control

Every transaction is recorded as income or expense and assigned to a category. The app starts with income categories such as rent, utilities, and other, and expense categories such as maintenance, utilities, insurance, taxes, and other, and you can add your own categories when the built-in list does not fit. Categories are organizational labels for your records, not tax advice.

## A portfolio view without the spreadsheet

When you own more than one rental, every property keeps its own transactions, monthly records, and documents inside the same app. You can switch between properties from the home screen, so the records for your whole portfolio live in one place instead of one spreadsheet per year. Monthly income, expenses, and net income are shown per property, which makes it easy to see how one building compares with another.

## Where spreadsheets get painful

Spreadsheets work, until they do not. Totals depend on formulas you wrote once and maintain forever, tabs multiply as years go by, and entering a receipt on your phone means opening the right file and scrolling to the right row. One wrong reference and the annual total is quietly wrong. For landlords who record income and expenses more than a few times a month, a purpose-built app removes that maintenance, because you record a transaction once and the monthly numbers are there.

## Start with the property that needs it most

You do not need to move everything at once. Add the property with the most activity, record the next few transactions as they happen, and check the monthly summary. When that feels natural, add the rest of your portfolio. The download button below takes you to the App Store.
