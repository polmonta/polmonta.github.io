## Gross versus net and what the difference means

Gross income is everything a property brings in before costs, including rent, late fees, and any other income you record. Net income is what remains after the expenses you record, such as maintenance, utilities, insurance, and taxes. The gap between the two is where most properties earn or lose money, which is why per-property net income matters more than the headline rent figure.

## The inputs behind the numbers

Every profitability number starts with a recorded transaction. For each property you record income and expenses with amounts, dates, and categories, and the app calculates monthly income, expenses, and net income from those transactions. You can also add recurring items for regular amounts, such as monthly rent or a monthly parking fee, so expected income is part of the picture you review.

## Comparing properties across your portfolio

When every property uses the same fields, comparing them is straightforward. Open each property's monthly summary and look at income, expenses, and net income side by side. A building with the same rent but twice the expenses stands out immediately, and the transactions behind that difference are a few taps away. For landlords growing beyond one property, see the [growing investors page](/for/growing-property-investors/).

## From monthly net income to ROI estimates

ROI puts net income in context with what you invested. A common way to think about it is annual net income divided by the cash you invested, expressed as a percentage. ManageState keeps the income and expense records that feed that calculation, but it does not calculate ROI or yield for you.

## Try the ROI calculator

When you are ready to estimate, the [rental property ROI calculator](/tools/rental-property-roi-calculator/) on this site applies standard yield formulas to the annual rent, expenses, purchase price, and cash invested that you enter. The results are estimates, not guarantees, and they depend on the numbers you put in.
