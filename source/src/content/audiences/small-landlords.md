## Problems small landlords run into

With one to five properties, most landlords do not have a back office. They have a phone, a pile of receipts, and a spreadsheet that grows messier every year. The common failure points are familiar. Rent payments scattered across bank statements, receipts lost in email, and year-end panic about whether the totals are right. The fix is not more software. It is records that stay organized as they are entered.

## A daily and monthly workflow

The workflow that works for small landlords is small. When money moves, record it. The tenant's rent arrives on the first and you add the income transaction with the rent category. The plumber finishes on the tenth and you add the expense with the maintenance category. Once a month you open each property's summary and review income, expenses, and net income. A concrete example. A landlord with two condos records about twelve transactions a month, entering each one from the phone.

## The features that fit one to five properties

ManageState matches the scale of a small portfolio. Per-property transactions and categories, monthly income and expense summaries, documents attached to the property they belong to, and an XLSX export when you need records in a spreadsheet format. There is no chart of accounts to configure and no team setup. The [financial tracking page](/features/financial-control/) walks through the income and expense workflow in detail, and the [document organizer page](/features/document-management/) covers keeping leases and receipts with the property.

## When enterprise software is excessive

Enterprise property-management platforms are built for larger teams, multiple roles, and complex workflows such as maintenance dispatch and portfolio reporting across dozens of buildings. For one to five properties, that scale brings setup time, training, and cost that the records themselves do not justify. ManageState is not an enterprise platform, and it does not try to replace one. It covers the records a small landlord actually keeps.
