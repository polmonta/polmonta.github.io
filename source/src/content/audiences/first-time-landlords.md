## A setup checklist for your first property

Getting organized takes an evening, not a course. Here is a checklist that works. Add the property and its details in the app, add a recurring item for the expected monthly rent, record the first rent payment as income with the rent category, upload the signed lease to the property's documents, and record the first expense, such as a repair or a utility you pay. After the first full month, open the monthly summary and review income, expenses, and net income. Each step uses the same record keeping the app is built around, so the habit you build on day one is the habit that carries through the year.

## Build an income and expense habit

The habit that keeps first-time landlords out of trouble is simple. Record on the day money moves. Rent arrives, add income. You pay for a repair, add the expense. You do not need to remember categories perfectly. Start with rent, maintenance, utilities, insurance, or taxes, and add your own categories later if you need them. The [financial tracking page](/features/financial-control/) shows the full workflow with an example.

## Documents from day one

The documents you collect in your first year are the ones you will need later. The signed lease, the inspection report from move-in, receipts for repairs, and correspondence that matters. Upload each one to the property as it arrives, and it stays with the property instead of in a folder you will search later. The [document organizer page](/features/document-management/) explains how document storage works.

## Watch your first property perform

After a few months you will have something new. Your first rental's performance in numbers. Each property's monthly summary shows income, expenses, and net income, so you can see how the first year is actually going. When tax time comes, the [tax export page](/features/tax-export/) explains how to export your records for your accountant.
