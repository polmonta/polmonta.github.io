## The 5–25 property stage

Somewhere between the fifth and twenty-fifth property, a single spreadsheet stops being a record system and becomes a liability. Tabs multiply, formulas drift, and one property's numbers get confused with another's. This is the stage where consistent, per-property records start to pay for themselves, not because the work is glamorous, but because you cannot manage what you cannot compare.

## Consistent records across every property

Consistency is the point. Every property in ManageState uses the same transaction fields. Income or expense, category, amount, and date. Monthly income, expenses, and net income are calculated per property from those transactions, and documents stay attached to the property they belong to. When every building is recorded the same way, comparing two properties means comparing the same kind of numbers.

## Per-property performance you can review

With five to twenty-five properties, the question is rarely whether the portfolio is fine and more often which property is dragging. Each property's monthly summary answers that directly with income, expenses, and net income per building and the transactions underneath. The [profitability page](/features/profitability-roi/) explains gross versus net and how to use the [ROI calculator](/tools/rental-property-roi-calculator/) to estimate yield when you are evaluating a purchase or a hold.

## Limits, stated honestly

ManageState is a records and documents tool for landlords, and it is worth stating what it is not. It does not collect rent payments, does not prepare tax returns, and does not provide investment, tax, or legal advice. It does not replace your accountant, your bank, or a property-management team. What it does is keep the records those people and you need, consistent and exportable.
