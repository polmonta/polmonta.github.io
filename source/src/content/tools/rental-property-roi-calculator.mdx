## What this calculator estimates

The calculator turns four numbers you enter into four estimates:

- **Net income (annual):** annual rent minus annual operating expenses.
- **Gross yield:** annual rent divided by the purchase price.
- **Net yield:** net income divided by the purchase price.
- **Cash on cash:** net income divided by the cash you invested.

Percentages are rounded to two decimals, and amounts appear in whatever currency you enter. The results are estimates, not guarantees: they are only as good as the numbers behind them. For the record-keeping that produces those numbers, see the [rental property ROI and profitability page](/features/profitability-roi/).

## What the results do not include

The estimates exclude financing, taxes, appreciation, vacancy, and jurisdiction-specific assumptions unless you entered them as operating expenses. That means a mortgage payment, depreciation, or a jurisdiction's specific rules are not part of the results on their own. If you have financing, the cash-on-cash estimate here does not reflect debt service, and if your jurisdiction applies rules you did not enter, the estimates will not match your actual outcome. Treat the results as a quick comparison tool, and ask an accountant or tax adviser before relying on any number for a purchase decision.

## Where the numbers come from

The four inputs are the same numbers a landlord already tracks per property: rent received, operating expenses paid, what the property cost, and the cash put in. ManageState keeps those records on iPhone, and the monthly summaries give you the annual figures to enter here. The app does not calculate ROI or yield for you; the calculator on this page does, using only the numbers you enter.

## What to do with the results

Use the estimates to compare one property with another using the same inputs, or to check whether a property you already own is performing the way you expected. Because the results exclude financing, taxes, appreciation, and vacancy unless you entered them, a property can look attractive here and still carry costs the calculator does not see. For landlords weighing their first properties or a growing portfolio, the [small landlords page](/for/small-landlords/) walks through the workflow of keeping the records these estimates depend on.
