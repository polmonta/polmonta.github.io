## What to record in a landlord expense tracker

Record every expense that belongs to a rental property: repairs, maintenance, utilities you pay, insurance premiums, supplies, and any other payment tied to the property. Each entry needs the amount, the date, and a category. The rule that keeps a tracker honest is simple. If money left your account for a property, record it. Small amounts count, because a year of small expenses adds up to a real number. For the full income and expense workflow, see the [financial tracking page](/features/financial-control/).

## A repeatable entry habit that sticks

The difference between a useful tracker and an abandoned one is the habit. Enter the expense on the day you pay it, not at the end of the month. Keep the entry short: open the property, add the expense, pick the category. When entry is that short, the habit survives. Landlords who record as they go end the year with records that match reality, while landlords who batch entries in January are reconstructing from memory.

## Categories are organization, not tax advice

Categories such as maintenance, utilities, insurance, and taxes are organizational labels that keep your records readable. They are not a statement about what you can deduct. What counts as a deductible expense depends on the rules for your situation and your jurisdiction, so treat categories as filing structure and let your accountant or tax adviser decide what to claim. The [tax export page](/features/tax-export/) explains how organized records become an export you can share.

## Attaching receipts and documents to the record

A number in a tracker is only half the record; the receipt is the other half. In ManageState, documents are stored with the property they belong to, so a receipt for a repair sits next to that property's transactions instead of in a folder you will search later. The [document management page](/features/document-management/) explains how uploads keep the document's name, type, size, and upload date with the property.

## A monthly review worth keeping

Once a month, open each property's summary and check the income, expenses, and net income for the month. The review catches double entries and missed expenses while they are still easy to fix, and it gives you the numbers behind decisions such as whether a rent increase is worth discussing. If you want a spreadsheet-style file to review or archive, the [rental income and expense template](/tools/rental-income-expense-template/) on this site provides a CSV you can fill in.
