## What to look for in a small-landlord property management app

Start from the records a small landlord actually keeps, not from a feature list. With one to five rentals you need per-property income and expenses, monthly totals you can review, documents that stay with the property they belong to, and an export when you want records in a spreadsheet format. You do not need team roles, maintenance dispatch, or portfolio reporting across dozens of buildings. If a tool cannot do the basics per property, the extras will not save you at year end.

## A workflow that fits one to five properties

The right test for a small landlord is the daily workflow. When the tenant's rent arrives, you add an income transaction with the amount, date, and category. When the plumber finishes, you add the expense with the maintenance category. Once a month you open each property's summary and review income, expenses, and net income. If that flow takes more than a few taps per transaction, it will not survive a full year. The [small landlords page](/for/small-landlords/) walks through this workflow for a portfolio of one to five properties.

## Essential features versus enterprise extras

Essential features are the ones you touch every month: per-property transactions, categories, monthly summaries, documents attached to the property, and an export you can share. Enterprise extras are built for larger teams and complex operations, such as role-based access, maintenance dispatch, and cross-portfolio reporting. For one to five properties, enterprise extras add setup time and cost without improving the records. The [comparison page](/compare/landlord-app-vs-spreadsheets/) shows where an app helps and where a spreadsheet may still be enough.

## Why iPhone-first matters for daily entry

Small landlords run their portfolios from a phone, so the app you choose should feel natural there. Entering a receipt in a parking lot, checking a monthly summary on the way home, and finding a lease during a phone call should all be quick. An app designed for a desktop with a mobile version bolted on tends to make daily entry painful. ## A decision checklist before you download

Before you commit, run through this checklist with any candidate app. Can it track income and expenses per property? Does it keep documents with the property they belong to? Are monthly totals calculated from what you record, or do you maintain formulas yourself? Can you export your records in a spreadsheet format? Does daily entry work from your phone? If the answer is no to more than one of these, keep looking. The [financial tracking page](/features/financial-control/) shows the workflow behind these answers in detail.
