## The income and expense model that stays accurate

A rental tracker works on one model. Every money movement is either income or expense, belongs to one property, and carries an amount, a date, and a category. Rent, late fees, and other money in are income. Maintenance, utilities, insurance, taxes, and other money out are expenses. From those entries the monthly numbers follow, and nothing depends on a formula you wrote once and must maintain forever. The [financial tracking page](/features/financial-control/) shows this model with a worked example.

## Per-property records keep properties comparable

When every property uses the same fields, comparing them is honest. Each property in ManageState keeps its own transactions and monthly records, so a building with the same rent but twice the expenses stands out immediately. You switch between properties from the home screen, and the records for your whole portfolio live in one place instead of one spreadsheet per year. This matters most as a portfolio grows past a few rentals, as the [growing investors page](/for/growing-property-investors/) explains.

## Reviewing cash flow month by month

Monthly totals are where tracking pays off. Open a property's summary and you see income, expenses, and net income for the month, calculated from the transactions you recorded. The review answers practical questions. Did the rent arrive? Did the month's repairs eat the margin? Is net income trending the way you expect? Checking once a month keeps problems small, because a missed entry is caught while it is still easy to fix.

## Common spreadsheet errors a tracker avoids

Spreadsheets fail in predictable ways. A row entered in the wrong year quietly skews the annual total. A formula dragged over the wrong range stops summing what you think it sums. A receipt kept in a folder but never entered leaves the numbers short. And tabs multiply until the file itself is hard to trust. A tracker removes that maintenance by deriving totals from entries. You record once, and the monthly numbers are there. The [comparison page](/compare/landlord-app-vs-spreadsheets/) covers where a spreadsheet may still be enough.

## How the ManageState workflow works

The workflow is the same every time. When the tenant's transfer lands, add an income transaction with the rent category. When you pay for a repair, add an expense with the maintenance category. Add a recurring item for regular amounts such as monthly rent, so expected income is part of the picture you review. At any point you can export an XLSX workbook with property, transaction, recurring-item, and monthly summary data, as the [tax export page](/features/tax-export/) explains. For the entry habit behind this workflow, see the [landlord expense tracker guide](/guides/landlord-expense-tracker/).
