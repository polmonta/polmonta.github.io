## Year-round organization beats a tax-time scramble

The purpose of a landlord tax record app is to make tax time boring. If income and expenses are recorded per property as they happen, the year-end picture is already assembled instead of reconstructed. Each transaction carries its amount, date, and category, and monthly income, expenses, and net income are calculated from what you record. The [financial tracking page](/features/financial-control/) shows this workflow in detail, and the [tax export page](/features/tax-export/) explains what happens to the records at year end.

## Exports you can share with your accountant

When your records are ready, ManageState exports an XLSX workbook with property, transaction, recurring-item, and monthly summary data. From your iPhone you share the file with anyone you choose, including your accountant, through the usual share sheet. The workbook is built from the records you entered, so the more complete your entries are, the more complete the export is. ManageState organizes and exports your records; it does not prepare your return.

## Record completeness is the real work

The app keeps the structure; the landlord keeps the completeness. A missing receipt or an expense entered under the wrong property shows up in the export as a gap or a misplaced row. The habit that keeps records complete is the same one as always. Record on the day money moves, attach documents to the property they belong to, and check each property's monthly summary once a month. The [expense tracker guide](/guides/landlord-expense-tracker/) covers the entry habit, and the [document organizer guide](/guides/rental-property-document-organizer/) covers keeping receipts with the property.

## Rules vary by jurisdiction

Tax rules for rental income and expenses vary by country, state, and your personal situation. What counts as a deductible expense, how depreciation works, and what records you must keep are questions with local answers. ManageState does not provide tax, legal, or accounting advice, and it does not determine what you can claim. Use the organized records to have an informed conversation with a qualified tax professional where you file.

## Questions to ask your accountant

Bringing organized records is only half of tax time; the other half is asking the right questions. Useful questions include: which of my records do you need for the return, does the XLSX export work for your workflow or do you prefer another arrangement, which expenses should I be recording more carefully, and what records must I keep and for how long. Your accountant answers those from your situation and your jurisdiction, and the records you kept all year make the answers concrete.
