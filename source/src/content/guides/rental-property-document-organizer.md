## What belongs in a rental property document inventory

Start with the records you have actually needed or wished you had: signed leases and rental agreements, receipts for repairs and supplies, inspection notes, insurance policies, and any correspondence that matters for a property. The inventory does not have to be exhaustive on day one. Add documents as they arrive, and keep the rule simple. If losing the document would cost you time or money later, it belongs in the property's records. The [document management page](/features/document-management/) explains how uploads work in ManageState.

## Naming and retrieval without hunting through folders

A document you cannot find is as useful as a document you never saved. In ManageState, documents live on the property's screen, so retrieval starts by opening the property, not by remembering a folder. When you upload, the document is saved with its name, type, size, and upload date, which is enough to tell one inspection report from another. Six months later, when the tenant asks about the paint condition noted at inspection, the report is right there with the date it was added. When you are ready to hand records to an accountant, the [tax export page](/features/tax-export/) explains how property records and documents fit together.

## Privacy practices for property documents

Property documents often contain personal details such as tenant names and lease terms, so it is worth knowing where they live. Document storage is part of your property records in your ManageState account. For details on what data the app collects and how analytics work, read the [privacy policy](/privacy/).

## A maintenance schedule you can keep

A maintenance schedule does not need to be elaborate to be useful. Keep a simple list of the recurring work for each property, such as seasonal inspections and appliance checks, and store the related documents as the work happens. Receipts for repairs, inspection reports, and service records all belong with the property, so the history is there when you need it. You keep the schedule itself in whatever way you already track your to-dos, and the documents that support each job stay on the property's screen.
