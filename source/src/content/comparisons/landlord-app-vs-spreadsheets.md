## Setup

A spreadsheet starts blank. You design the columns, write the formulas, and keep the file somewhere you will not lose it. ManageState starts with a property. Add the property, then record income and expenses as they happen, and monthly totals are calculated from what you enter. For a one-off budget, a spreadsheet is faster to start. For records you will keep for years, the setup cost is similar either way, and the difference shows up in maintenance.

## Mobile entry

This is where spreadsheets quietly get expensive. Entering a receipt on a phone means opening the right file, scrolling to the right row, and trusting yourself not to break a formula. ManageState is built for entry from the phone. You add a transaction to the property in a few taps with amount, date, and category. If you record while standing in a parking lot after a repair, that difference is real.

## Document association

A spreadsheet stores numbers, not documents. Landlords end up with a spreadsheet plus a folder of PDFs plus an email trail, and the link between a receipt and its row is memory. ManageState stores documents with the property they belong to, so a lease or receipt lives next to the transactions it relates to. For landlords who need to find a document later, that association is the point.

## Calculations

Spreadsheets calculate with formulas you write and maintain. One wrong reference and the annual total is quietly wrong. ManageState calculates monthly income, expenses, and net income from the transactions you record, so the totals always match the entries. Neither tool computes ROI or gross yield for you. For that, use the [ROI calculator](/tools/rental-property-roi-calculator/) on this site.

## Exports

A spreadsheet is already an export format. You can send the file as it is. ManageState exports an XLSX workbook containing property, transaction, recurring-item, and monthly summary data, which is useful when you want a file to review, archive, or share with an accountant. Both tools give you a file. The difference is what it took to keep it accurate.

## Maintenance

Spreadsheet maintenance is ongoing. New year, new tabs. New expense type, new column. New formula, new chance to break something. ManageState maintenance is entry. Record transactions, and the structure takes care of itself. The trade-off is that a spreadsheet is infinitely flexible while an app has a fixed shape, flexibility you pay for every time you maintain it.

## When a spreadsheet may still be enough

A spreadsheet may still be enough when your volume is low, your records are simple, and you already have a system that works. Examples include one property with a handful of transactions a year, a landlord who genuinely enjoys building formulas, or a team that needs a shared file that lives outside any one app. The honest test is whether your current spreadsheet stays accurate with the effort you are willing to put in. If it does, keep it. If it needs rescuing every few months, that is the problem to solve.

## A fair conclusion

Neither tool is the right answer for every landlord. ManageState removes spreadsheet maintenance for landlords who record regularly from their phone and want documents beside the numbers, and a spreadsheet remains a legitimate choice for low volume and simple records.
